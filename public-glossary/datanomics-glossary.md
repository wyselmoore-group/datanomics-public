# Datanomics Public Glossary

This glossary defines the canonical terms of datanomics — the economic science
of how data and knowledge create, transfer, and accumulate value. Terms are
defined by Matthew Wysel and are maintained here as the single authoritative
reference for datanomics vocabulary. For the operational application of these
principles within the Knowledge-First Operations™ framework, see the
[KFO Framework Reference](../kfo-framework/kfo-framework-reference.md) in this
repository. For commercial application, see
[madewithdata.io/knowledgefirstoperations](https://www.madewithdata.io/knowledgefirstoperations).

---

**Club Theory** *(applied to data governance)*
Applied to data sharing ecosystems: a framework for analysing how platform
sponsors manage shared data resources as club goods — excludable but non-rival
within the membership. Club theory provides the basis for quantitative data
governance thresholds that prevent overgrazing of shared data libraries. When
new technologies alter the value each member receives from participation,
club governance mechanisms are required to sustain the health of the shared
data commons. (Wysel, Baker & Banks, 2023)

**Conditional Network Effects**
Network effects arising from data sharing in which the value generated depends
not only on the number of participants but on the conditions under which data
is shared — including supply level, supply frequency, and participant behaviour.
Distinct from unconditional network effects because value accrues asymmetrically
depending on how agents participate, not merely whether they do. (Wysel, 2023,
Ch. 5)

**Data as Currency**
One of three modes in which data generates value. As currency, data is exchanged
by individuals and firms to offset real-world costs or gain access to services,
operating as a medium of exchange independent of its informational content.
(Wysel, 2023, Ch. 3)

**Data as a Good**
One of three modes in which data generates value. As a good, data is the
exchangeable asset in both ad hoc and established marketplaces, traded between
parties with defined exchange value. (Wysel, 2023, Ch. 3)

**Data as a Resource**
One of three modes in which data generates value. As a resource, data is the
primary production input of intermediary businesses, applied internally to
generate insight and operational advantage. (Wysel, 2023, Ch. 3)

**Data Enrichment**
The process by which raw or available data is transformed into higher-value
insight through the removal of noise, reduction of uncertainty, and alignment
with community goals. Theorised by inverting the logic of Shannon's
communication system (Shannon, 1948): where Shannon's model encodes messages
for transmission through a noisy channel, the data enrichment model works in
reverse — decoding value from noise to realise data's capacity to resolve
uncertainty. Data enrichment is the development stage between extraction and
payoff realisation, operating within the data sharing platform. (Wysel, Baker
& Billingsley, 2021)

**Data Sharing Ecosystem**
A system comprising a community of agents, a shared data resource, and an
analytic system, connected across three layers: Asset Layer (data and system),
Management Layer (community organisation and value allocation), and Output
Layer (valuable interaction). Value creation from data requires all three layers
to be simultaneously present and functioning. (Wysel, Baker & Billingsley, 2021)

**Data Sharing Platform**
The operational system that connects the layers of the platform stack — data,
system, and community — with pertinent management tasks to create value from
data. Value is created from data only when each component of the data sharing
platform is present; the absence of any single component is sufficient to
suppress value creation entirely. (Wysel, Baker & Billingsley, 2021)

**Datanomics**
The economic science of how data and knowledge create, transfer, and accumulate
value. Datanomics draws from platform economics, information economics,
communication theory, and the study of data as a production factor and
exchangeable service. The field addresses the mechanisms by which value is
accrued to data, the conditions under which knowledge markets form and fail,
and the governance structures required for data sharing ecosystems to function
at optimal efficiency. Term coined in Wysel, Baker & Billingsley (2021) as
"agricultural datanomics." Generalised and extended in Wysel (2023).
(Wysel, Baker & Billingsley, 2021; Wysel, 2023)

**Extraction Value**
The value realised from a single use or transaction of a data or knowledge
asset. Treats data as a private, rivalrous good that is depleted or transferred
through exchange. Organisations that manage data exclusively for extraction
value systematically undervalue their data assets by failing to account for
extensibility, non-rivalry, and conditional network effects. (Wysel, 2023)

**Extensibility Value**
The value created when a data or knowledge asset is reused, recombined, and
compounded across multiple applications and stakeholders. Extensibility value
treats data through a service-dominant logic: exchange extends the asset rather
than depleting it. The gap between an asset's extensibility value and its
currently realised extraction value represents the structural opportunity cost
of goods-dominant data management. (Wysel, 2023)

**Goods-Dominant Logic** *(applied to data)*
The treatment of data as a private, rivalrous good — where exchange depletes
the asset and value is realised once through a single transaction.
Goods-dominant logic systematically undervalues data because it cannot account
for non-rivalry, conditional network effects, or extensibility. Widespread
treatment of data through goods-dominant logic creates predictable problems for
participants in data trading markets. (Wysel, 2023, Ch. 5)

**Information Chain Failure**
A vertical form of market failure occurring in data supply chains when the
conditions for value creation from data are not met at one or more stages of
the chain. Unlike horizontal market failures, information chain failure
propagates vertically: a failure at any upstream stage suppresses value
creation at all downstream stages, regardless of conditions at those stages.
(Wysel, 2023)

**The Invisible Market**
The set of knowledge and data markets that firms already operate but have not
made visible, governed, or commercially optimised. Every decision, conversation,
and AI interaction involves the exchange of knowledge in a market that most
organisations cannot see.

**Knowledge Asset**
A unit of knowledge that has been sufficiently formalised — through
documentation, codification, or structured capture — to be invested, multiplied,
traded, and managed as capital within and across organisational boundaries.
Knowledge assets are distinct from latent knowledge in that they have been
extracted and made investable; they are distinct from data in that they carry
embedded interpretation and organisational context. (Wysel, 2023)

**Knowledge Market Failure**
A condition in which the knowledge markets operating within or around a firm
produce systematically suboptimal outcomes — through mispricing, invisibility,
or governance failure — resulting in value stasis, leakage, or underinvestment
in knowledge assets. Knowledge market failure is the firm-level manifestation
of the broader datanomics problem. See also: Information Chain Failure;
Tragedy of the Data Commons. (Wysel, 2023)

**Latent Data**
Available data that has not yet been extracted, enriched, or applied to create
value. Latent data carries a shadow value — the potential value it would
generate if appropriately invested and developed — which is distinct from its
current realised value of zero. The existence of latent data is the primary
indicator of underinvestment in data enrichment infrastructure. (Wysel, 2023,
Ch. 3)

**Non-Rivalry** *(of data)*
The property of data by which one agent's use of a data asset does not diminish
its availability to other agents. Non-rivalry is a foundational property
distinguishing data from conventional private goods, and is the primary
economic basis for the service-dominant logic treatment of data. Non-rivalry
means that the efficient allocation rule for data differs from that of physical
goods: sharing does not require sacrifice. (Wysel, 2023, Ch. 5)

**Parallel Payoff**
The simultaneous realisation of multiple value streams from a single data or
knowledge investment: internal operational improvement, internal use of insight,
and external sale of insight. The parallel payoff model demonstrates that a
single decision to invest in data enables a firm to sell its data, use it to
improve other products and services, and incorporate it as data-enabled
learning — simultaneously and without depletion. (Wysel, 2023, Ch. 4)

**Service-Dominant Logic** *(applied to data)*
The treatment of data as a service rather than a good — where exchange extends
the asset rather than depleting it, and value is created through collaboration
and recirculation rather than single extraction. Under service-dominant logic,
sharing value to create data becomes strategically rational, enabling an
expanded Pareto frontier in data sharing ecosystems and making oversharing
rational under conditions where extensibility value exceeds extraction value.
(Wysel, 2023, Ch. 5)

**Shadow Value of Latent Data**
The estimated future value that latent data would generate if appropriately
developed and applied. The gap between shadow value and current realised value
represents the opportunity cost of underinvestment in data enrichment and
knowledge development — and is the primary basis for calculating the NPV of
knowledge infrastructure investments. (Wysel, 2023, Ch. 3)

**Tragedy of the Data Commons**
A form of market failure in data sharing ecosystems analogous to the classical
tragedy of the commons: when individual agents optimise unilaterally for
extraction from a shared data resource, the shared resource degrades and all
agents lose. Occurs when technological adoption alters the value each member
receives from sharing data, threatening the long-term supply of data, continued
membership of stakeholders, and ultimately the viability of the shared data
library. Preventable through club governance mechanisms that align individual
incentives with collective data health. (Wysel, Baker & Banks, 2023)

**Uncertainty-Coupled Valuation**
The valuation approach for data that accounts for both the uncertainty of
relevance and the uncertainty of utility inherent in a dataset. By inverting
the logic of Shannon's communication system, uncertainty coupling demonstrates
that the intrinsic value of data originates from its capacity to resolve
uncertainty rather than from its content alone. Uncertainty-coupled valuation
explains how data creates value as a resource, a good, and a currency, and
provides the theoretical basis for valuing data assets independent of context
or surrounding business model. (Wysel, 2023, Ch. 3)
