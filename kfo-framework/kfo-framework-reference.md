# Knowledge-First Operations™ — Framework Reference

This document defines the operational terms of the Knowledge-First Operations™
(KFO) framework. These terms operationalise the datanomics theoretical
framework (Wysel, 2023) within the Knowledge-First Operations™ methodology.
They are derived from PhD-originated constructs and developed through
MadeWithData diagnostic practice.

For the canonical academic definitions of datanomics theory, see the
[Datanomics Public Glossary](../public-glossary/datanomics-glossary.md)
in this repository. For commercial application, see
[madewithdata.io/knowledgefirstoperations](https://www.madewithdata.io/knowledgefirstoperations).

## Knowledge Roles

**Knowledge Creator**
The organisational role responsible for generating and validating new
proprietary knowledge inputs. Governs what knowledge enters the organisation
and determines relevance filtering at the point of creation. A strong knowledge
creator function is necessary but not sufficient for high knowledge asset
turnover — creation without downstream recirculation produces value stasis.

**Knowledge Investor**
The organisational role responsible for directing time, capital, and attention
toward high-yield knowledge development and governing reinvestment cycles. A
missing or weak knowledge investor role is the primary structural cause of
suppressed knowledge asset turnover; without investment discipline, knowledge
creation cannot compound.

**Knowledge Multiplier**
The organisational role responsible for standardising, packaging, and
integrating knowledge for reuse across teams, markets, and contexts. Operates
through models, processes, and frameworks that reduce uncertainty and increase
the reach of existing knowledge. The knowledge multiplier converts individual
insight into organisational infrastructure.

**Market Broker**
The organisational role responsible for extending internal knowledge into
external and parallel markets. Activates secondary market opportunities that
cannot be reached through internal application alone. A weak market broker
function is a reliable indicator of suppressed extensibility value: the
organisation creates knowledge it does not trade.

**Knowledge Trader**
The organisational role responsible for creating impact from the application of
knowledge, both internally and externally. Manages the exchange rate between
knowledge and operational or financial value. Knowledge trading is the point
at which intellectual capital generates measurable commercial return.

**Payoff Manager**
The organisational role responsible for measuring realised value from knowledge
deployment, ensuring lessons are reinvested, and prioritising how knowledge is
applied, sold, or used across cycles. Without an active payoff manager function,
organisations cannot distinguish between knowledge investments that compound
and those that produce one-time extraction only.

## Diagnostic Indices

**Knowledge Asset Turnover**
The rate at which intellectual capital converts into standardised, reinvestable,
and scalable value across an organisation's operations. High knowledge asset
turnover indicates systematic recirculation of learning; low turnover indicates
value stasis or leakage downstream of creation. Knowledge asset turnover is the
primary operational metric for diagnosing knowledge market health within a firm.

**Knowledge Asset Valuation**
The process of assessing the economic value of a knowledge asset, accounting
for its current extraction value and potential extensibility value across
multiple payoff pathways. Knowledge asset valuation is the precondition for
managing knowledge as capital rather than as a cost or byproduct of operations.

**Knowledge Impact Efficiency**
The proportion of potential knowledge value that is successfully commercialised
through an organisation's knowledge production process. Knowledge impact
efficiency is the primary quantitative indicator of invisible market losses
within a firm. Diagnostic observation of this metric forms part of the
MadeWithData Knowledge-First Operations™ assessment.

**Multiplication:Recirculation Ratio**
A diagnostic index expressing the relationship between an organisation's
capacity to create and multiply knowledge (upstream functions) and its capacity
to reinvest, trade, and capture payoffs from that knowledge (downstream
functions). A ratio significantly above 1:1 indicates a high-yield,
low-circulation configuration — the most common structural pattern in
organisations experiencing knowledge market failure.

**Weighted Knowledge Maturity**
A composite index of organisational competence across the six knowledge
commercialisation roles — Creator, Investor, Multiplier, Broker, Trader, and
Payoff Manager — scored and weighted to produce a single maturity indicator.
Used diagnostically to identify the specific functional gaps suppressing
knowledge asset turnover and to benchmark against best-in-breed organisations.
